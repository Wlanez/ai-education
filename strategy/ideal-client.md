# Ideal Client

## Initial profile

A company has an automation or AI agent in development or production, and an operational owner can describe a failure that affects customers, money, data, compliance, or staff workload.

The first season stays open across industries. Qualify the operational problem before choosing a vertical.

## Strong fit signals

- The workflow already handles real or soon-to-be-real business work.
- A failure has occurred, or the team can name a credible failure mode.
- The impact is observable: lost or duplicate records, delayed work, incorrect actions, increased API cost, customer friction, or inability to investigate.
- Someone can provide a safe, high-level workflow description and make time for a review conversation.
- The company is willing to test changes and inspect logs or other evidence.

## Useful questions for a first conversation

1. What business process does the automation support?
2. What unexpected event has caused it to behave incorrectly or stop?
3. How did the team discover the problem?
4. What happened to the customer, operation, or cost?
5. What evidence can be reviewed safely?

Do not request credentials, personal data, production payloads, or confidential materials during an introductory conversation.

## Lower-fit situations

- The requester wants a generic tool tutorial or a prompt list.
- There is no real or planned business workflow to assess.
- The only success measure is views, novelty, or a model benchmark.
- The requester expects a guarantee before the workflow and evidence are understood.
- The problem requires access or information the company cannot safely share.

## Qualification outcome

A conversation is qualified when the company has a relevant workflow, a specific reliability concern, an identifiable operational consequence, and a person who can discuss next steps. Record the reason and source in the scorecard without storing sensitive details.
